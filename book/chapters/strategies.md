## The Oldest Record Of An Option

Here's a famous anecdote shared by Aristotle about Thales of Miletus; an ancient Greek philosopher of Asia Minor. Thales was poor, to the point that people made fun on him: how could he pretend to be wise if he couldn't help himself out of poverty? Thales was hurt, but still was "too good" to earn a living in the usual time-consuming manner. Instead, he would watch the stars (philosophy in ancient times implied all sorts of sciences, including astronomy). Supposedly, something in the stars[^24] informed him that the next olive harvest (almost a year later) would be distinctly great. So he decided to spent the little money he had in all[^25] the olive mills of Miletus and Chius. He couldn't of course rent them (let alone buy them), being poor. All he could receive for his money was the right to rent them, if so he decided later, at a particularly high rent. The deal seemed like a no-brainer for mill owners: they got paid to promise to rent their mills at a high price!
